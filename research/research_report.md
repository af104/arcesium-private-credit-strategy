# Arcesium should own private credit's data layer

Arcesium's best path in private credit is to act as the neutral data and fund-operations system of record that loan servicers, monitoring tools and AI agents plug into. It should not try to become a fund administrator or rebuild the point tools other vendors already sell. The company now has the pieces for this. It has a data platform (Aquata), an operations platform (Opterra), an AI layer (Arcesium Intelligence, May 2026), a front-office system (Limina, bought Feb 2026) and a loan-ops partner (Hypercore, Jun 2026). Its one named private credit win is asset-based finance at Neuberger Berman, not direct lending. The market is big and still growing: about **$3.5T of private credit AUM at end-2024, up 17%**, and **$4.1T in 2025** by the AIMA/ACC count. The software wallet is much smaller. My estimate is roughly $0.4-1.0B a year, and that number is derived, not sourced. Competition is getting tougher. Clearwater bought its way into private credit and went private for $8.4B. SS&C pitches software plus administration as an AI cost advantage. Fund admins and data giants are buying the small AI loan-ops startups. The four places Arcesium can push hardest are asset-based finance at loan-tape scale, auditable valuation and data lineage, AI agents for operations rather than underwriting, and locking in its loan-ops partner layer before a rival buys it. Two caveats up front: Arcesium does not publish revenue or client counts, and no public source breaks down private credit managers by AUM tier. The revenue model therefore rests on clearly flagged estimates.

## Arcesium spent 2023-2026 building a front-to-back stack

Arcesium was founded in March 2015 in New York as a joint venture of the D. E. Shaw group, backed by Blackstone's hedge fund solutions arm. D. E. Shaw and Blackstone Alternative Asset Management were its first clients ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcesium); [WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/buy-side-technology/news/2397661/de-shaw-baam-found-post-trade-technology-firm)). **J.P. Morgan took a strategic stake in January 2020** after it started using Arcesium for alternative fund administration in 2018 ([Arcesium](https://arcesium.com/press-release/j-p-morgan-takes-stake-in-tech-company-for-alternatives)). The company is private and reports no revenue. Its own press release boilerplate is the best scale signal. Gross AUM on the platform went from **$4.3T+ with 2,000+ staff in January 2025** ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250115179753/en)) to **$6.4T+ with 2,400+ staff in April 2026** ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260407557995/en)). That is about 49% asset growth and 20% headcount growth in 15 months. Gross AUM moves with leverage and markets, though, so it is a rough proxy for revenue, not a stand-in for it.

The product sequence shows a clear plan. Arcesium launched **Aquata**, a cloud data platform, in April 2023. It then packaged its operations tools as **Opterra** in July 2024, covering reconciliation, treasury and collateral, investor accounting, regulatory reporting and private markets operations ([AlternativesWatch](https://www.alternativeswatch.com/2024/07/16/arcesium-launches-new-operational-platform-opterra/)). In October 2025 it added the **Aquata Marketplace**, with pre-built connections to 20 partners including Accelex ([Business Wire via Mammoth Times](https://business.mammothtimes.com/mammothtimes/article/bizwire-2025-10-7-arcesium-unveils-aquata-marketplace-to-power-data-connectivity-elevate-workflows-and-accelerate-ai-readiness-for-institutional-investors)). In December 2025 it gave Aquata a generative AI agent that pulls structured data out of PDFs and emails, plus an MCP server so clients can query Aquata data from any enterprise AI tool ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251215394851/en)). On **February 2, 2026** it made its first acquisition, **Limina**, a Stockholm portfolio and order management system. CEO Gaurav Suri called it "a watershed moment" and the start of a "front-to-back" platform ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/fintech/arcesium-acquires-limina-to-deliver-a-unified-front-to-back-investment-platform/)). On about **May 20, 2026** it launched **Arcesium Intelligence**, an AI layer on AWS Bedrock with 100+ pre-built agents (for example P&L explain, NAV review and portfolio monitoring), an Agent Studio for building custom agents, and a model-agnostic orchestration layer ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260520686508/en/); [ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/arcesium-launches-comprehensive-ai-platform-to-operationalize-agentic-ai-for-institutional-investment/)). The Jobright page's item "Arcesium launches agentic AI platform" (Hedgeweek, May 22, 2026) is the same launch.

For private credit, Arcesium's product page covers **direct lending, asset-based finance (consumer, residential, receivables, fund finance, equipment leasing) and synthetic risk transfers**. That includes loan-level covenants and cash flow schedules, the full loan lifecycle (accruals, paydowns, rollovers, defaults, restructurings), daily loan-tape ingestion and LP dashboards. **Managed Services** handles outsourced loan-tape cracking, reconciliations and NAV production. The page does not present Arcesium as a loan servicer or fund administrator. It describes API links to the servicers, trustees and administrators that do that work ([Arcesium](https://www.arcesium.com/private-market-managers-investors)).

Named private credit proof points are thin. No joint clients were named ([TMCnet/PR Newswire](https://www.tmcnet.com/usubmit/-hypercore-announces-technology-integration-with-arcesium-comprehensive-private-/2026/06/02/10392783.htm)). ## A $4T market with a sub-$1B software wallet

Pick one definition of the market and stick with it. The gap between definitions is bigger than any growth assumption. The broad AIMA/ACC measure put global private credit at **$3.5T at end-2024, up 17%**, with **$592.8B deployed in 2024 (up 78%)**, **65% of AUM in the US**, and **asset-backed lending at 16% of deployment** ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/research-and-markets/global-private-credit-market-reaches-us3-5-trillion-aum-threshold-report/)). A later ACC headline puts **2025 AUM at $4.1T** ([AIMA](https://www.aima.org/article/press-release-global-private-credit-market-hit-us-4-1tn-aum-in-2025-acc-data-reveals.html)). Narrower "private debt fund" counts are lower: Preqin forecast **$2.64T by 2029 at a 9.88% CAGR** ([Alternative Credit Investor](https://alternativecreditinvestor.com/2024/09/18/private-debt-aum-to-hit-2-64tn-by-2029/)). BlackRock ([Alternative Credit Investor](https://alternativecreditinvestor.com/2025/04/07/blackrock-predicts-private-credit-aum-will-reach-4-5tn-by-2030/)) and Preqin's 2030 report ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251016075672/en/Preqin-Releases-Private-Markets-in-2030-Report)) both point to **$4.5T by 2030**.

The fastest-growing piece is the one that needs the most operations technology. **Evergreen private credit reached $503B, up 27%**. Perpetual non-traded BDCs hit $170B, and BCRED alone was over $70B ([With Intelligence](https://www.withintelligence.com/insights/private-credit-funds-surpasses-500bn/)). These vehicles need daily or monthly NAV and investor-level accounting. The State Street and Apollo daily-NAV deal in October 2026 shows where this is going ([State Street via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/state-street-advances-daily-nav-113000964.html)). There are headwinds too. Non-traded BDC fundraising fell in Q4 2025 ([Blue Vault](https://bluevaultpartners.com/nontraded-bdc-fundraising-falls-returns-rally-in-q4-2025/)), and PwC reports redemptions and gating at some semi-liquid funds ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/industries/private-equity/private-credit-survey.html)).

Concentration matters most for a vendor's target list. The **top 20 managers hold 36% of dry powder** ($138B of $385B), and 17 of them are US-based ([S&P Global MI](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2025/1/top-20-private-credit-managers-hold-more-than-one-third-of-dry-powder-86886642)). The **top 20% of surveyed managers did about 85% of 2024 deployment**, and ACC has **250 member managers holding over $2T** ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/research-and-markets/global-private-credit-market-reaches-us3-5-trillion-aum-threshold-report/)). The eight large listed alternatives managers held **$2.33T of credit AUM** in 2024, led by Apollo at $616B. That figure includes some public credit ([Preqin](https://www.preqin.com/news/credit-aum-at-listed-alternatives-managers-rises-21-5-to-usd2-33tn-in-2024)). Preqin counted **1,345 private debt funds in market** seeking $490.4B, and emerging managers raised only 6% of 2024 capital ([Alternative Credit Investor](https://alternativecreditinvestor.com/2025/03/19/largest-managers-and-funds-increasingly-dominate-private-credit/)). No public source counts managers by AUM tier, so the tier counts below are my own power-law estimate built from those anchors. They should be checked against Preqin Pro or PitchBook before the memo goes out.

For pricing, the best public proxies are vendor averages. **Clearwater had $841M ARR, 2,500+ clients and 152 clients above $1M ARR**, which works out to about $336K average ARR per client, with 98% gross and 109% net retention ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260218581398/en/)). **Allvue has over $200M ARR growing above 15%** ([Reuters via WHBL](https://whbl.com/2026/08/21/buyout-firm-vista-explores-3-billion-sale-of-private-markets-data-provider-allvue-sources-say/)) and says it has 500 clients ([Allvue](https://www.allvuesystems.com/news/allvue-debuts-onevue-with-the-launch-of-portfolio-intelligence-and-deal-analytics-the-industrys-first-benchmarks-built-from-private-credit-deal-data/)), or roughly $400K per client. Small-fund administration quotes run about **$75-200K a year for $50-250M funds and $200-500K+ above $250M**, but that benchmark is VC-oriented and has a disclosed conflict of interest ([VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/fund-admin-pricing-benchmark)). On outsourcing, **94% of North American private fund managers use third-party administrators** to some extent ([Ocorian](https://www.ocorian.com/news-press-releases/outsourcing-takes-bigger-role-north-american-fund-managers)), and **51% of GPs plan to outsource more** ([Business Wire, CSC](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250430162117/en)).

## Ten rivals, three business models, no clear winner in credit ops

The field splits by delivery model. **Pure software** vendors (Arcesium, Clearwater, Allvue, Broadridge) sell technology only. **Software plus services** players (SS&C, State Street, BNY, Juniper Square, the fund admins) bundle technology with administration or custody. **Specialists** own one slice of the workflow, such as loan operations, monitoring, valuation or document extraction. Arcesium's most useful selling point against the bundlers is neutrality: it does not administer funds and it holds no positions. Against Clearwater, the argument has to be depth of data platform and handling of complex credit.

**SS&C** is the broadest and most hostile rival. Its private credit stack includes Precision LM (loan origination and servicing), Global Debt Manager (agency), GlobeOp administration, Advent Geneva accounting, EVOLV loan accounting and DealCentre AI. It claims $830B in private markets assets under administration and 500+ clients ([SS&C](https://ssctech.com/industry/private-markets/private-credit)). On its October 22, 2026 call, management said owning the whole fund-administration stack lets it deploy AI faster and has raised its win rate. It reported **900+ agentic workflows and 200 AI agents**, guided to **$6.74-6.78B of 2026 revenue**, and said it would make a large acquisition now that SaaS valuations have fallen ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/ss-c-technologies-holdings-inc-030238243.html)). The two companies have a legal history. Arcesium sued SS&C Advent over Geneva licensing in 2020 ([PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/arcesium-files-lawsuit-against-ssc-advent-for-illegal-and-anticompetitive-activities-301072814.html)), and the court dismissed those antitrust claims in April 2021 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/antitrust/ss-c-ducks-more-antitrust-claims-over-geneva-accounting-software)). SS&C's trade secrets claims against Arcesium were dismissed with prejudice, and on August 20, 2025 the court denied SS&C's motion to vacate that judgment ([CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10660690/ssc-technologies-holdings-inc-v-arcesium-llc/)). SS&C filed an appeal to the Second Circuit on August 26, 2025, outcome not found ([PacerMonitor](https://www.pacermonitor.com/public/case/59792004/SSC_Technologies_Holdings,_Inc_v_Arcesium_LLC)). A separate SS&C suit against D. E. Shaw survived a motion to dismiss ([Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/6843a45183cc715f894ab1aa)).

**Clearwater** is now the closest like-for-like rival. In 2025 it bought **Enfusion for $1.5B** (front office, aimed at hedge funds) ([FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/m-a/clearwater-analytics-to-go-private-in-8-4bn-acquisition)). It also bought **Beacon (~$560M)** and **Blackstone's private credit tool Bistro ($125M)**, so it has Blackstone and PIMCO as reference users ([Clearwater](https://cwan.com/press-releases/clearwater-analytics-to-acquire-beacon-and-blackstones-bistro-to-deliver-investment-intelligence-across-private-markets/)). Permira and Warburg Pincus closed an **$8.4B take-private on June 25, 2026** and named AI as a priority ([Permira](https://www.permira.com/news-and-insights/announcements/clearwater-analytics-completes-84-billion-take-private-acquisition-by-permira-and-warburg-pincus)). Clearwater officially reported 800+ AI agents available for deployment in November 2025 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251105092732/en)); executives have since cited "nearly 1,000." Its new owners carry leverage, so expect them to push hard to cross-sell into credit managers.

**State Street** agreed to buy Mercatus on July 21, 2021 and said it would launch Alpha for Private Markets on top of it, covering private debt among other asset classes ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210721005497/en); [Private Funds CFO](https://www.privatefundscfo.com/state-street-buys-mercatus-platform-to-automate-private-markets/)). In October 2026 it moved part of **Apollo's private credit business from monthly to daily NAV** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/state-street-advances-daily-nav-113000964.html)). That is the strongest evidence that administrators have the edge in the evergreen segment. **BNY** is reorganizing around platforms and building its Eliza AI for internal use ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/node/7952565); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/09/29/bny-unveils-updated-eliza-platform-as-it-touts-that-majority-of-employees-use-ai-tools-daily)), and it markets Public & Private Credit Solutions, covering fund administration and accounting, custody, CLO trustee and agency, loan closing and administration, and middle office for credit funds, CLOs and managed accounts ([BNY](https://www.bny.com/content/dam/bnymellon/documents/pdf/bny-public-private-credit-solutions-transcript.pdf)). That is a services offering, not a software platform that competes head-on with Arcesium. **FIS** relaunched its Private Capital Suite as cloud SaaS in September 2025, aimed at private equity, not credit ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250923635099/en/FIS-Launches-Enhanced-Private-Capital-Suite-to-Power-the-Future-of-Private-Equity)). **Broadridge Sentry** is a real private credit and CLO front-to-middle tool, with **Carlyle (December 2023)**, Pemberton, MUFG and Northwall as clients ([IBS Intelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/carlyle-taps-broadridge-sentry-to-manage-deal-pipeline-loan-operations)). It is not a book of record.

**Allvue** is the most direct software rival in credit. ## Four openings where Arcesium's data position wins

**First, asset-based finance at loan-tape scale.** PwC's 2026 survey ranks ABF, senior direct lending and specialty finance as the most attractive segments ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/industries/private-equity/private-credit-survey.html)). ABF was already 16% of 2024 deployment. The specialist field is small: Cardo AI calls itself "the operating system for asset-based finance" on a $15M Series A ([Cardo AI](https://cardoai.com/announcements/cardo-ai-announces-15-million-series-a-funding-co-led-by-blackstone-fintop-capital-and-jam-fintop/)), and Finley is now inside a servicer. Arcesium already has the reference client (Neuberger Berman) and the high-volume loan data muscle. ABF also supports pricing by data volume, which lifts ACV. This is the strongest "push next" option because it builds on something Arcesium has already proven.

**Second, auditable valuation and loan data lineage.** EY found that private credit teams spend "a significant amount of time manually curating data sets," that the industry lacks "universal identifiers," and that a single data layer is the fix ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_us/insights/wealth-asset-management/data-strategy-in-private-credit)). Regulatory attention on marks is rising. Fitch put 2025 private credit defaults at a record 9.2%, and commentators flag an independence problem when valuation tools are owned by firms that hold the assets ([AltsTech](https://altstech.com/private-credit-is-funding-the-machinery-that-watches-private-markets/)). Arcesium holds no positions. A versioned, auditable book of record for loan data and valuation inputs is a neutral role that SS&C, State Street and investor-backed monitoring tools cannot claim as easily.

**Third, AI for operations, not underwriting.** Lumonic's 2026 survey of about 150 lenders found AI adoption roughly doubled to about 50%, but loan monitoring and portfolio management tool adoption stayed flat. Its summary: "AI changes how lenders source. Not yet how they operate" ([Lumonic](https://www.lumonic.com/content/2026-the-state-of-private-credit-technology)). PwC found **only 16%** of credit managers call AI-enabled portfolio monitoring a current priority ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/industries/private-equity/private-credit-survey.html)). Read one way, that is a weak market. Read the other way, nobody owns ops AI in credit yet. Arcesium's notice-extraction agent and its NAV-review and portfolio-monitoring agents are aimed at this gap. PwC also flags weak default and restructuring capability and a need for real-time early warning. Combining Hypercore's covenant-breach flags with Arcesium's portfolio data would give credit managers an early-warning and workout product that is still open space.

**Fourth, secure the loan-ops layer.** Arcesium deliberately does not do loan servicing or administration. That keeps it neutral, but it leaves the company dependent on partners in a layer that is being bought up quickly. Tenor went to Juniper Square, Finley to Concord, Canoe to Bloomberg. Hypercore, with a $13.5M Series A led by Insight Partners ([Insight Partners](https://www.insightpartners.com/blog/hypercore-raises-13-5m-series-a-led-by-insight-partners-to-launch-ai-admin-agent-for-private-credit/)), is one of the few independent options left. The Limina deal shows Arcesium will buy adjacent modules. A deeper commercial tie with Hypercore, a minority stake, or an acquisition would protect this layer. The case against buying is fair too: owning loan ops could unsettle the administrators and servicers Arcesium integrates with.

Two other options are weaker. **Evergreen and daily NAV** is a fast-growing segment, but State Street's Apollo deal shows administrators win there because they own the valuation and NAV process. Arcesium should sell into it alongside administrators, not against them. **Private credit front office**, meaning deal pipeline and origination, is an obvious gap because Limina serves public multi-asset markets. But Allvue, Broadridge Sentry and SS&C DealCentre already compete there. Buying Allvue at up to $3B would close the gap in one move, but no source shows Arcesium has the capacity or interest. Treat it as a scenario to watch, not a recommendation.

## Conclusion

The main point for the memo is that competition in private credit operations has shifted from features to ownership. Admins, data giants and private equity sponsors are buying the point tools and bundling them with services. That makes a neutral, open data platform more valuable to the large and mid-size managers that want to choose their own servicers, administrators and AI tools. Arcesium's growth in this market depends less on closing every product gap and more on making Aquata and Opterra the place where loan-level truth lives, while the partner network stays independent.
